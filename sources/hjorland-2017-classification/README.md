## What is distinctive

The pragmatist account of classification: no neutral classification exists; every scheme serves some purpose and view.

## Notes

Checked 2026-11-25 against the stored IEKO version of the article (section numbers are IEKO's).

- **Definition** (§1, after Bliss 1929): a classification is "a series or system of classes arranged in some order according to some principles or conception, purpose or interest". For most purposes "classification", "taxonomy" and "systematics" can be taken to mean the same thing (§2).
- **Four approaches** (§4.2c): α **rationalism** (classes by logical division from first principles, as in Ranganathan), β **empiricism** (classes from observed similarity, as in numerical taxonomy), γ **historicism** (classes reflect development and theory: the periodic table became "truly natural" only once the elements' real relations were known), δ **pragmatism / critical theory**.
- **His position, δ**: classifications are "constructed in order to support explicit interests"; "a classification can never be neutral, but will always tend to support certain goals and interests at the expense of other interests". Quoting Nobes and Stadler: "objectivity is neither possible nor desirable in classification. Despite the arbitrariness, some classifications can be more reasonable or more useful than others." Shera's tree: an organism to the botanist, a source of income to the lumberman; "it is the external relations, the environment, of the concept that are all-important in the act of classifying".
- **Against "it is all arbitrary"**: pragmatism here is long-term as well as short-term (cladistics serves long-term interests), and "truth is always the goal in science"; the criteria for classes have to be argued, not assumed.
- **Similarity is not enough** (§4.2c on empiricism): which features count as "alike" needs a theory or purpose.
- **"Natural" classification** (§4.3a): long contested. Bather (1927): "not a single naturalist had a clear idea of what he meant by 'natural'. All he knew was that the other fellow's classification was unnatural."
- For us: write the purpose down first (the meta-characteristic), say whose interests the vocabulary serves (Jonathan's and the agents'), and judge the taxonomy against that purpose, not against an idea of the natural one.
