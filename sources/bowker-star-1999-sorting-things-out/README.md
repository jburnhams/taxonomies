## Notes

- A classification is "a spatial, temporal, or spatio-temporal segmentation of the world". In the ideal it has consistent, unique principles; mutually exclusive categories; and completeness. "No real-world working classification system that we have looked at meets these 'simple' requirements and we doubt that any ever could." *(Quotes from prior knowledge; check against ch. 1.)*
- Cases: the International Classification of Diseases, nursing interventions, race under apartheid, tuberculosis.
- Ideas we use: classifications become **invisible** infrastructure; **residual categories** ("other", "not elsewhere classified") show where the scheme fails; **torque** is the strain when a thing's real path does not fit its category; **boundary objects** are things (and vocabularies) that are loose enough to be shared by groups and tight enough to mean something in each.
- For us: design for the misfit. Every facet needs a way to say "doesn't fit yet" that is reviewed, not a permanent "other".
