## What is distinctive

Boundary objects: how groups with different vocabularies still work together.

## Notes

Checked 2026-11-25 by reading the stored scan (pp. 392-393, 409-411; no usable text layer, so quotes are transcribed by eye).

- **Two devices** let amateurs, collectors, trappers, administrators and scientists work together at Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology without one shared worldview: **methods standardisation** and **boundary objects** (p. 392).
- **Definition** (p. 393): boundary objects "both inhabit several intersecting social worlds ... *and* satisfy the informational requirements of each of them. Boundary objects are objects which are both plastic enough to adapt to local needs and the constraints of the several parties employing them, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites. They are weakly structured in common use, and become strongly structured in individual-site use." They "have different meanings in different social worlds but their structure is common enough to more than one world to make them recognizable". The first pass quoted this from memory; the wording matches.
- **Four types** (pp. 410-411), "not an exhaustive list":
  1. **Repositories**: ordered piles of objects indexed in a standardised way (a library, a museum); people use or borrow from them for their own purposes.
  2. **Ideal types**: an abstract description (a diagram, atlas, the idea of a species) that fits no one case exactly and is "fairly vague", so adaptable everywhere.
  3. **Coincident boundaries**: objects with the same boundaries but different internal contents (California mapped as roads and campsites by amateurs, as "life zones" by biologists).
  4. **Standardised forms**: methods of common communication across dispersed work (the collectors' field form), which delete local uncertainty.
- For us: the shared kernel of cross-domain terms should be a boundary object: weakly structured in common use, strongly structured inside each domain. A glossary term shared across repos is an *ideal type* (vague on purpose at the top); the term templates are *standardised forms*.
