## Notes

Checked against the stored book 2026-11-25. Page numbers are the book's.

- **Essays**: Reuse Versus Compression; Habitability and Piecemeal Growth; Abstraction Descant; The Quality Without a Name; Pattern Languages; The Failure of Pattern Languages; then essays on languages, business and life.
- **Habitability**: "the characteristic of source code that enables programmers, coders, bug-fixers, and people coming to the code later in its life to understand its construction and intentions and to change it comfortably and confidently." Gabriel says it is more than clarity. What blocks it is "overdesign, overabstraction, and the beautiful, taut monument of software"; piecemeal growth is "a reality".
- **The Failure of Pattern Languages** (pp. 57-60). This is the essay that matters most for us. By Alexander's own account, quoted from Grabow (1983), the pattern language did not produce the quality it described. Buildings designed with it (the Modesto Clinic, projects drawn from bootleg copies) "basically looked like any other buildings of our time", while their designers believed them radically different. Alexander: the books "do quite a good job of identifying the difference but actually do not accomplish anything. The conceptual structures ... are just not deep enough". His response was to turn to the **process** (mortgages, zoning, construction, the roles of architect and builder): "By controlling the process, you control the result". Gabriel draws the software parallel: system structure follows the structure of the organisation that builds it, and quality follows the process.
- For us: a pattern catalogue or vocabulary does not by itself change what gets built. The build method and the review loop (who checks, when, against what) do. Patterns and glossary terms name the target; the process has to hit it.
