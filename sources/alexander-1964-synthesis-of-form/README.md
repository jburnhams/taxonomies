## What is distinctive

Alexander's mathematical start: decompose a design problem by clustering the requirements that interact.

## Notes

The book itself is not stored; these notes were checked on 2026-11-25 against Gabriel's 2023 commentary, which quotes the 1971 preface in full (Appendix E.2).

- Design is achieving **fit** between form and context; list the possible **misfits**, link the ones that interact, and decompose the graph into nearly independent clusters. Each cluster gets a "diagram", the ancestor of a pattern. The decomposition was computed by programs Alexander wrote for the IBM 7090 (quoted in the commentary).
- In effect a classification method (numerical clustering of requirements), and the root of the idea that you name the recurring clusters.
- **1971 preface, confirmed**: "once the book was written, I discovered that it is quite unnecessary to use such a complicated and formal way of getting at the independent diagrams ... you can create, and develop, these diagrams piecemeal, one at a time, in the most natural way, out of your experience". Readers "have focused on the method which leads to the creation of the diagrams, not on the diagrams themselves, and have even made a cult of following this method." He rejects "design methods as a subject of study, since I think it is absurd to separate the study of designing from the practice of design". In 1971 he said of the Design Methods field: "forget it, forget the whole thing".
- Gabriel adds that "A City is Not a Tree" (1965) repudiates the tree decomposition the book relies on.
- For us: two cautions. Do not make a cult of the build method (keep it light and done inside real work, not as a separate exercise), and build the vocabulary piecemeal, one term at a time, out of use.
