## What is distinctive

The founding text of faceted classification: analyse a subject into independent facets, then combine them, instead of enumerating every compound class in one tree.

## Notes

- **Facets.** A subject is split into a few independent aspects (facets), each with its own list of values (foci). Compound subjects are built by combining one value from each facet in a fixed **citation order**, rather than by pre-listing every combination (the "enumerative" way of Dewey and LCC).
- **PMEST**: five fundamental categories, cited in order of "decreasing concreteness": **P**ersonality, **M**atter, **E**nergy, **S**pace, **T**ime (confirmed in the ISKO entry on facet analysis).
- **Three planes**: the idea plane (the concepts), the verbal plane (the words), the notational plane (the codes). Deciding what the categories are is a different job from naming them and from numbering them. This split is the most useful single idea for us.
- **Canons** for characteristics of division (differentiation, relevance, ascertainability, permanence) and for arrays (exhaustiveness, exclusiveness, helpful sequence). Spiteri (1998) reduced them to a usable checklist; see [`../spiteri-1998-facet-model/`](../spiteri-1998-facet-model/).
- Criticism (Hjørland, ISKO): rationalist and a priori, with "lack of empirical basis" and "speculative ordering of knowledge".
