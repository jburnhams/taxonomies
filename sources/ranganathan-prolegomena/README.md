## What is distinctive

The founding text of faceted classification: analyse a subject into independent facets, then combine them, instead of enumerating every compound class in one tree.

## Notes

Checked 2026-11-25 against the two stored texts: the 1937 first edition of *Prolegomena* and the 6th edition (1960) of *Colon Classification*. Both are OCR, so quotes are cleaned of OCR errors. The 1967 third edition, where the three planes are set out, is not stored.

- **Facets.** A subject is split into a few independent aspects (facets), each with its own list of values (foci). Compound subjects are built by combining one value from each facet in a fixed **citation order**, rather than by pre-listing every combination (the "enumerative" way of Dewey and LCC). The word "facet" does not appear in the 1937 *Prolegomena*; it is everywhere in the 1960 *Colon Classification* (410 hits).
- **PMEST**, confirmed in *Colon Classification* 6th ed., rule 05501: "The five Fundamental Categories are given below in the increasing sequence of concreteness": Time [T], Space [S], Energy [E], Matter [M], Personality [P], with connecting symbols `.` `.` `:` `;` `,`. Cited in practice from P to T (decreasing concreteness). Energy "usually consists of problem, action, etc."; Ranganathan admits there is not "yet" an exact criterion for recognising it. PMEST is not in the 1937 edition.
- **Three planes** (idea, verbal, notational): not in either stored text; from the 1967 edition (prior knowledge, confirmed only by the ISKO entry). Still the most useful single idea for us: deciding what the categories are is a different job from naming them and from numbering them.
- **Canons, 1937 edition** (pp. 30-92). For characteristics of division: Differentiation, Concomitance, Relevance, Ascertainability, Permanence, Relevant Sequence, Consistency. For arrays: Exhaustiveness, Exclusiveness, Helpful Order, Consistent Order. For chains: Intension, Modulation. Spiteri (1998) reduced these to a usable checklist; see [`../spiteri-1998-facet-model/`](../spiteri-1998-facet-model/).
- **Canons of Terminology, 1937** (pp. 62-72), new to these notes and directly about naming:
  1. **Currency**: each term "must be the one in current use among those who specialise in the universe to which the scheme is applicable", and "there should be some arrangement by which the terms can be changed over to current ones, as and when changes take place".
  2. **Reticence**: terms "should not be critical"; "use only terms which are purely descriptive and not opinionative". His examples are Dewey's "Humbugs" and "Minor Authors"; "the colourless descriptive word 'Other'" would have served.
  3. **Enumeration**: a term's meaning is fixed by the classes listed under it, because people and schemes do not agree on what terms denote.
  4. **Context**: a term's meaning is fixed by its place in its chain (the classes above it), because "one and the same term denotes several different entities".
- Criticism (Hjørland, ISKO "Facet analysis", checked): the approach's "main weaknesses are (1) its lack of empirical basis and (2) its speculative ordering of knowledge".
