## What is distinctive

Why a strict tree is the wrong shape for most real structures, and why designers keep drawing trees anyway. The same argument as facets, made by an architect.

## Notes

- **Tree**: a collection of sets where "for any two sets that belong to the collection either one is wholly contained in the other, or else they are wholly disjoint".
- **Semilattice**: "when two overlapping sets belong to the collection, the set of elements common to both also belongs to the collection". Every tree is a (trivial) semilattice.
- Scale: on 20 elements a tree can hold at most 19 further subsets; a semilattice can hold more than a million.
- Example: at a Berkeley corner, a drugstore, a traffic light and a newsrack form a unit (people waiting at the light read the papers); the newsrack is also part of the drugstore. The units overlap.
- Why designers make trees: "the mind has an overwhelming predisposition to see trees wherever it looks"; in redrawing experiments people remove overlaps.
- But "overlap alone does not give structure. It can also give chaos." The right overlaps matter.
- For us: a taxonomy forced into one tree will lose real overlaps. Facets, polyhierarchy and cross-links are the semilattice; rules about which overlaps are allowed stop the chaos.
