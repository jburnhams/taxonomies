## What is distinctive

The clearest comparison of the shapes a classification can take, and what each demands of your knowledge.

## Notes

Four structures, each with what it requires and where it breaks:
- **Hierarchy**: strict is-a, inheritance, mutually exclusive classes, transitivity. Needs full knowledge of the domain and one agreed principle of division. Breaks when things belong in two places.
- **Tree**: like a hierarchy but the relation can be part-whole or other, without inheritance. Same rigidity.
- **Paradigm**: a two-way grid (two facets crossed). Shows empty cells, which point at things not yet found or not named. Limited to two or three dimensions.
- **Facets**: several independent dimensions. Flexible, needs no complete theory of the domain, but gives no single view and the facets have to be chosen well.
Her point that matters for us: each structure encodes a different amount of **theory** about the domain. A strict hierarchy claims you understand it fully; facets claim less. Pick the structure your knowledge can support.
