## What is distinctive

253 named, linked solutions, from regions down to details, written so non-architects can design with them.

## Notes

- Each pattern "describes a problem which occurs over and over again in our environment, and then describes the core of the solution to that problem, in such a way that you can use this solution a million times over, without ever doing it the same way twice."
- Fixed form: number and **name**; asterisks (none, one, two) for how sure the authors are; a picture; a paragraph on the larger patterns it helps complete (context); the **problem** in bold; the evidence; "**Therefore:**" and the solution in bold; the smaller patterns that complete it.
- Ordered from large to small (towns, buildings, construction), but linked as a **network**: each pattern points up and down to others. Not a tree.
- It is called a *language* on purpose: the names are the vocabulary, the links are the grammar, and a design is a sentence. The names are short noun phrases you can say in a meeting (LIGHT ON TWO SIDES OF EVERY ROOM, SIX-FOOT BALCONY).
